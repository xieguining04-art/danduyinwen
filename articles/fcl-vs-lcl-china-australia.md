Compare FCL and LCL shipping by cargo volume, handling, transit planning, destination charges and total landed cost. This guide explains the main decisions and cost components an importer should confirm before booking.

## 1. FCL vs LCL: what is the difference?

### FCL — Full Container Load

With FCL, one shipper books the whole container. The cargo is loaded at
a factory, warehouse or container yard in China and the container
normally stays sealed until destination handling requires it to be
opened.

Common equipment includes **20GP**, **40GP** and **40HQ**.

### LCL — Less than Container Load

With LCL, cargo from several shippers is consolidated into the same
container. Your goods are delivered to a consolidation warehouse in
China, loaded with other shipments, then deconsolidated at the
destination warehouse in Australia.

| Factor               | FCL                            | LCL                                    |
|----------------------|--------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Space                | Whole container                | Shared container space                 |
| Typical pricing      | Per container                  | CBM / revenue ton plus handling        |
| Origin handling      | Container loading              | Warehouse receiving and consolidation  |
| Destination handling | Container release and delivery | Deconsolidation and warehouse handling |
| Cargo handling       | Usually fewer handling points  | Usually more handling points           |
| Best fit             | Larger or sensitive shipments  | Smaller commercial shipments           |

## 2. Which is cheaper: FCL or LCL?

For small shipments, LCL is usually cheaper because the importer only
pays for the space used instead of booking an entire container.

As cargo volume grows, the calculation changes. LCL costs often increase
with each CBM while many FCL charges are container-based. At some point,
the total LCL cost can approach or exceed the cost of a 20GP.

Compare:  
**LCL ocean freight + origin CFS/warehouse + destination
deconsolidation + delivery**  
versus  
**FCL ocean freight + container/local charges + container delivery**

This is why the cheapest ocean freight line on a quotation does not
necessarily produce the cheapest shipment.

## 3. At what CBM does FCL become cheaper than LCL?

There is no fixed CBM threshold that works for every China–Australia
shipment.

As a practical planning rule, once cargo reaches around **12–15 CBM**,
many importers should ask for both an LCL quote and a 20GP FCL quote.
The actual break-even point can be lower or higher depending on:

- Cargo weight and density
- Chinese origin city and consolidation warehouse
- Australian port
- LCL minimum charges
- Destination unpack and warehouse fees
- Current 20GP container rate
- Final delivery postcode
- Special handling requirements

**Do not use 12–15 CBM as an automatic booking rule.** Use it as the
point where you start comparing both complete quotations.

## 4. Australian destination charges can change the answer

Destination charges are especially important when comparing LCL and FCL.

LCL may involve charges for deconsolidation, warehouse handling, release
documentation and delivery from the destination CFS. FCL may involve
shipping-line local charges, container release, terminal/depot
arrangements and container delivery.

Always compare the **total destination cost**, not only the China-side
freight.

For a deeper cost breakdown, see our [Australia destination charges

## 5. FCL vs LCL transit time

The vessel transit itself may be similar if both shipments use the same
sailing, but the total door-to-door time can differ.

LCL normally requires additional time for:

- Delivery into the consolidation warehouse
- Waiting for container consolidation
- Destination deconsolidation
- Warehouse release

FCL can therefore be operationally faster and more predictable for
larger shipments, although actual timing depends on carrier schedules,
customs and biosecurity.

## 6. Cargo handling, damage risk and control

LCL cargo is handled more frequently because it must be received,
consolidated, unloaded and separated from other shipments. Good
packaging is therefore particularly important.

FCL generally offers more control over loading and cargo securing. This
can be valuable for:

- Furniture and cabinets
- Fragile products
- Machinery
- Oversized cartons
- High-value commercial cargo

FCL does not eliminate cargo risk. Correct blocking, bracing, weight
distribution and moisture protection are still essential.

## 7. 20GP vs 40HQ for FCL shipping

A **20GP** is often suitable for dense or heavy cargo, while a **40HQ**
is commonly chosen for larger-volume cargo.

| Container | Typical use                                                   | Key consideration                                  |
|-----------|---------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| 20GP      | Heavy machinery, dense building materials, smaller full loads | Weight may become the limit before volume          |
| 40GP      | General full-container cargo                                  | Lower internal height than 40HQ                    |
| 40HQ      | Furniture, cabinets, packaging, high-volume mixed goods       | Better cubic capacity for lighter/voluminous cargo |

Actual loading capacity depends on packaging dimensions and loading
method. A theoretical CBM figure is not the same as usable loading
capacity.

## 8. Which option is better for different cargo types?

### Furniture & cabinets

FCL often becomes attractive because the cargo is bulky, and fewer
handling points can reduce damage risk.

### Building materials

Dense cargo may suit a 20GP. Weight distribution and container payload
need to be checked carefully.

### Small commercial orders

LCL is often the practical choice when volume is low and the importer
wants to avoid paying for unused container space.

### Machinery & sensitive equipment

FCL may provide better loading control, but dimensions, centre of
gravity and securing requirements must be assessed.

## 9. Can multiple Chinese suppliers share one FCL container?

Yes. Multiple suppliers can send goods to a China consolidation
warehouse before loading one 20GP or 40HQ.

A consolidation service can include:

- Supplier pickup or warehouse receiving
- Carton counting and basic condition checking
- Labelling and sorting
- Document coordination
- Loading plan preparation
- Container loading

This can be more efficient than shipping several separate LCL
consignments when the combined volume becomes large enough.

## 10. Example decision scenarios

### Scenario A: 4 CBM, 700 kg

LCL would usually be the first option to price because the cargo volume
is small.

### Scenario B: 13 CBM, 3,500 kg

This is a good point to compare LCL against a 20GP. Destination LCL
charges can materially affect the decision.

### Scenario C: 28 CBM of furniture

FCL is normally worth serious consideration because the shipment is
bulky and handling control matters.

### Scenario D: 55+ CBM of lightweight cabinets

A 40HQ may offer much better cost efficiency than LCL, subject to actual
carton dimensions and loading feasibility.

These examples are planning illustrations, not fixed price rules. Every
shipment should be quoted on current rates and exact packing data.

## 11. How to compare an FCL and LCL quotation correctly

Ask both quotations to use the same:

- China pickup/origin scope
- Australian destination
- Cargo volume and weight
- Customs clearance scope
- Final delivery postcode

| Check                   | LCL quote                 | FCL quote                         |
|-------------------------|---------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Origin pickup           | Included?                 | Included?                         |
| Origin handling         | CFS / warehouse fees      | Container/loading/local fees      |
| Ocean freight           | CBM / RT basis            | Per container                     |
| Australia local charges | Deconsolidation / release | Shipping-line / container charges |
| Customs & biosecurity   | Included or excluded?     | Included or excluded?             |
| Delivery                | CFS to final postcode     | Container delivery / unload plan  |
| Transit time            | Including consolidation?  | Direct or transshipment?          |

## 12. Frequently asked questions

### Is FCL or LCL cheaper from China to Australia?

LCL is usually more economical for smaller shipments, while FCL can
become more cost-effective as volume increases. The correct choice
depends on total origin, ocean freight, destination handling and
delivery costs rather than ocean freight alone.

### At what CBM should I compare FCL and LCL?

There is no universal break-even point. As a practical rule, once cargo
reaches roughly 12–15 CBM, many importers should compare a 20GP FCL
option against LCL. The actual break-even point changes by route,
weight, cargo type and destination charges.

### Is FCL faster than LCL from China to Australia?

FCL is often operationally simpler at origin and destination because the
cargo does not need consolidation and deconsolidation. LCL may require
extra warehouse handling, so total door-to-door time can be longer even
when the vessel transit is similar.

### Which is safer for fragile or high-value cargo, FCL or LCL?

FCL usually gives the importer more control because the cargo is handled
fewer times and does not share container space with unrelated shipments.
Correct packing and securing are still essential.

### Can I consolidate cargo from several Chinese suppliers into one FCL container?

Yes. Multiple suppliers can deliver cargo to a China warehouse for
checking, consolidation and container loading before export, provided
the documentation and loading plan are coordinated correctly.

### Should I choose 20GP or 40HQ for FCL shipping to Australia?

A 20GP is often suitable for dense or heavy cargo, while a 40HQ is
commonly used for higher-volume cargo such as furniture, cabinets and
mixed consumer goods. The best choice depends on cargo dimensions,
weight and loading efficiency.

## Final recommendation

Use LCL when the shipment is genuinely small. As volume grows, compare
the complete LCL landed logistics cost against a 20GP or 40HQ FCL
solution. The best answer is based on current rates, destination
charges, cargo handling and total delivery cost—not one fixed CBM rule.
