In one enquiry about equipment for Nigeria, the customer had received an earlier shipping and packaging quote of approximately **USD 14,115**. The buyer wanted a more economical option and needed to understand what would be included. TengYoda proposed a breakbulk option alongside a preliminary container estimate and discussed equipment preparation, insurance and destination responsibilities.

## From enquiry to a shipping plan

These are planning stages, not a record of completed transport.

1. **Understand:** Pickup, freight and delivery requirements.
2. **Review cargo:** Cab removal, dimensions and weights.
3. **Compare:** Container and breakbulk scopes.
4. **Plan handover:** Discharge, clearance and inland delivery.

## 1. What did the customer need?

The customer planned to move industrial equipment from an inland Chinese factory to Lagos, Nigeria. The enquiry involved wheeled loading equipment, tracked machinery and a crushing unit.

The customer asked for a quotation and advice covering:

1. Collection from the factory and inland transport in China.
2. Packing and loading arrangements.
3. Ocean freight to Nigeria and estimated transit time.
4. Cargo insurance and an explanation of its coverage.
5. Available destination-port and clearance information.
6. A comparison of container shipping and other practical options.

The buyer wanted to understand the full cost and responsibilities before committing to a plan.

## 2. Why did the customer seek another option?

The customer confirmed that the earlier quotation of approximately USD 14,115 related to this equipment and was described as covering shipping and packaging. The buyer wanted to explore whether the cost could be reduced.

The supplier had suggested container transport. The customer asked whether partial dismantling could allow the machinery to fit into one 40-foot high-cube container. Before answering, the loading team would need the final dimensions and weights, a feasible stuffing and securing plan, and the costs of dismantling and reassembly.

**Adding the machines' cubic metres together is not enough to establish that they will fit.** Individual pieces must be checked against the actual container opening, internal space, payload and loading method.

## 3. What did TengYoda propose?

To address the buyer's cost concerns and wish to limit dismantling, TengYoda proposed a breakbulk option for comparison with the preliminary container estimate.

In the initial comparison, the amount listed for TengYoda's ocean freight and origin-port services was roughly 30% below the amount of the earlier quotation. **The figures did not cover the same services.** The earlier amount was described as shipping and packaging; the compared TengYoda figure did not include waterproof or anti-rust protection, and inland transport and other items needed to be considered separately.

That difference is therefore an **initial comparison of differently scoped amounts**, not a confirmed 30% saving on the full shipment. A valid comparison requires the same cargo condition, services and handover points.

## 4. Can the machinery travel without further dismantling?

The customer confirmed that the cabs of the loading equipment and the tracked machine are to be removed. One reason for considering breakbulk was to assess whether the main bodies could travel in that condition without further dismantling merely to fit a standard container.

If the manufacturer, carrier and ports accept the actual dimensions, weight and handling method, avoiding additional dismantling may reduce reassembly work and associated costs at destination. It does not eliminate the need to reinstall the cabs or establish a fixed saving.

Before booking, the parties still need to confirm:

- The transport dimensions and gross weight of each machine after cab removal.
- How each detached cab will be protected, packed and identified.
- Whether the remaining main bodies can be accepted without further dismantling.
- Lifting arrangements and the final package list.
- What work, tools and support will be needed to restore the equipment at destination.

Acceptance of the proposed shipping condition remains subject to the actual cargo review.

## 6. How should the full costs be compared?

Use the same cargo list, pickup location, destination and handover points for each option. Do not treat an unknown charge as zero.

| Stage | Questions to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Factory preparation | Who pays for dismantling, protective packing and loading onto the pickup vehicle? |
| China inland transport | Who arranges the vehicle, route and any special handling or permissions required? |
| Export and origin port | Are export documents, declaration, port handling, loading and lashing included? |
| Ocean freight | What are the rate basis, validity, surcharges and carrier acceptance conditions? |
| Destination port | Where does included discharge end, and who pays local handling, transfer or storage? |
| Import and local transport | Who arranges clearance, duties and taxes, pickup, delivery and unloading? |
| Reassembly and insurance | Are they separately quoted, and what conditions or exclusions apply? |

Only after the missing items have been priced and the scopes aligned can the buyer calculate the actual difference between the options.
