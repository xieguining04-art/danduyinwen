Understand DDP shipping responsibilities, quotations, customs, GST, biosecurity and delivery risks before importing from China to Australia. This guide explains the main decisions and cost components an importer should confirm before booking.

## 1. What does DDP mean?

Under **Incoterms® 2020**, DDP means **Delivered Duty Paid**. The seller
delivers the goods when they are placed at the buyer's disposal at the
named destination, **cleared for import and ready for unloading**. The
seller bears the costs and risks of bringing the goods to that point
and, where applicable, handles export, transit and import formalities
and pays import duties and applicable taxes.

This makes DDP the Incoterms rule with the **maximum seller
obligation**. The named place matters. “DDP Australia” is too vague; the
commercial agreement should identify a practical destination such as a
warehouse address or clearly defined delivery point.

**Important:** DDP defines the cost and risk allocation between seller
and buyer. It does not override Australian customs, GST,
product-compliance or biosecurity law.

For the official Incoterms explanation, see the ICC Incoterms® 2020 DDP guidance.

## 2. DDP seller vs buyer responsibilities

| Stage                       | Seller under DDP                                                                     | Buyer                                                                                  |
|-----------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| China factory / pickup      | Arrange agreed origin movement and bear the cost                                     | Provide order and delivery information                                                 |
| China export clearance      | Seller responsibility                                                                | Assist with information if reasonably required                                         |
| International freight       | Seller arranges and pays                                                             | Normally no freight booking responsibility                                             |
| Australian import clearance | Seller is commercially responsible for arranging a compliant import-cleared delivery | May need to provide information or assistance depending on the lawful import structure |
| Duty / import GST           | Commercially for seller's account under DDP                                          | Should confirm GST-credit treatment and importer identity before shipment              |
| Final delivery              | Seller bears cost/risk to the named destination                                      | Take delivery; unloading is normally for the buyer unless otherwise included           |

The biggest practical issue is therefore not “who books the freight?” It
is **how the seller will lawfully complete Australian import clearance
and tax obligations**.

## 3. Who is the importer on a DDP shipment to Australia?

Australian Border Force states that import declarations are made by the
importer/owner or by a **licensed customs broker acting on the owner's
behalf**. For commercial consignments over AUD 1,000, an Import
Declaration is generally required before the goods can be released into
home consumption.

A DDP seller outside Australia should therefore be able to explain the
actual structure:

- Who will appear as the owner/importer on the Australian import
  declaration?
- Which licensed customs broker will lodge the declaration?
- If the seller is a non-resident, is there a properly documented
  Australian resident-agent or other compliant arrangement?
- Whose ABN/GST registration, if any, is being used?
- Who is legally liable for import GST and duty?

ABF notes that only the owner of the goods or a licensed customs broker
can submit an import declaration for home consumption. See ABF import declaration guidance.

**A useful buyer question:** “Please show me who will be listed as the
owner/importer on the Australian entry and how import GST will be
handled.” A professional DDP provider should be able to answer this
clearly before booking.

## 4. GST, customs duty and the GST-credit issue

Under a true DDP sale, the seller is commercially responsible for
applicable Australian import duty and GST. But the **legal importer and
GST-credit position** still need to be structured correctly.

Australian GST on a taxable importation is generally 10% of the value of
the taxable importation, which can include customs value, duty,
transport and insurance to the place of consignment in Australia. The
entity that makes the taxable importation is responsible for the import
GST.

For Australian businesses registered for GST, one of the most important
DDP questions is whether the business will be entitled to claim an
**input tax credit** for import GST. ATO guidance makes clear that
GST-credit entitlement depends on who actually makes the creditable
importation; simply reimbursing GST paid by an unrelated third party
does not automatically give the Australian buyer a GST credit.

**Why this matters:** An importer may accept a “DDP tax included” price,
then later discover that the import GST was paid under another entity
and cannot be claimed as the buyer expected. For GST-registered
Australian businesses, this can materially change the real landed cost.

## 5. DDP does not remove Australian biosecurity requirements

Australia's biosecurity rules apply regardless of the Incoterm used. The
Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry uses **BICON** to set
commodity-specific import conditions. Depending on the product,
requirements can include permits, declarations, certificates,
inspections or treatment.

DDP quotations should clearly address who bears additional costs if the
cargo is:

- selected for biosecurity inspection
- held for treatment or cleaning
- packed with non-compliant timber
- missing an import permit or supporting declaration
- contaminated with soil, plant material, insects or other biosecurity
  risk material

Check product-specific conditions in the official
BICON system before the goods leave China.

## 6. What should a DDP price from China to Australia include?

A professional DDP quotation should be more specific than “USD X
delivered.” Ask for the scope in writing.

| Cost area             | Items to confirm                                                                                                  |
|-----------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| China origin          | Factory pickup, trucking, warehouse handling, export customs, documentation, container/LCL handling               |
| International freight | FCL, LCL or air freight; carrier surcharges; direct or transshipment routing                                      |
| Australia destination | Shipping-line/local charges, terminal/depot handling, cargo release or EDO-related charges                        |
| Customs               | Brokerage, import declaration, duty, GST, import processing charges                                               |
| Biosecurity           | Routine processing plus whether inspection, treatment, storage or permit costs are included or excluded           |
| Delivery              | Delivery postcode, vehicle type, tail-lift/crane/forklift requirements, waiting time and unloading responsibility |
| Container risk        | Free time, detention/container hire, demurrage/terminal storage and damage liability                              |

## 7. DDP vs DAP: which is better for an Australian importer?

| Issue                         | DDP                                                      | DAP                                                                           |
|-------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Main transport to destination | Seller                                                   | Seller                                                                        |
| Australian import clearance   | Seller's responsibility                                  | Buyer's responsibility                                                        |
| Duty / import GST             | Seller's account                                         | Buyer's account                                                               |
| Importer control              | Lower if seller controls the import structure            | Higher for the Australian buyer                                               |
| GST-credit visibility         | Must be checked carefully                                | Often clearer for a GST-registered Australian importer handling its own entry |
| Best fit                      | Seller has a proven compliant Australian import solution | Buyer wants control of customs, duty/GST and broker relationship              |

DDP is not automatically “better” because more is included. For
established Australian companies, DAP can sometimes create a cleaner tax
and customs trail because the buyer remains directly involved in the
import declaration.

## 8. When DDP can make sense

### First-time importers

DDP can reduce coordination work if the seller's Australian import and
delivery structure is transparent and proven.

### Predictable general cargo

Standard, non-regulated goods with clear classification and low
biosecurity risk are easier to quote reliably.

### Smaller door-to-door shipments

A properly structured LCL or air DDP service can simplify supplier
pickup through final delivery.

### Buyer wants one landed price

DDP can simplify budgeting when the quotation clearly states inclusions,
exclusions and the named delivery point.

## 9. Common DDP risks and red flags

Before accepting a DDP quotation, look for these warning signs:

- **No importer explanation:** the provider cannot tell you who will be
  named on the import declaration.
- **“Tax included” but no tax documents:** there is no clear evidence
  showing how GST/duty is paid.
- **Buyer ABN used without approval:** never allow your company identity
  to be used casually for another party's import structure.
- **Biosecurity costs vaguely excluded:** “DDP” is quoted but
  inspection, treatment and storage are all passed back to the buyer
  without explanation.
- **Incorrect or low customs values:** undervaluation can create
  penalties, delays and compliance risk.
- **Wrong product description or HS classification:** the cheapest
  channel is not useful if the customs declaration is inaccurate.
- **No destination detail:** the quotation says “Australia door
  delivery” without a postcode, unloading method or access conditions.

**Price is not the only risk.** A suspiciously cheap DDP quote may
simply move customs, tax or compliance risk somewhere the buyer cannot
see. Ask for the structure, not just the total price.

## 10. Typical DDP shipping process from China to Australia

Supplier / Factory → China Pickup → Warehouse or Container Loading →
China Export Declaration → Ocean / Air Freight → Australian Arrival →
Import Declaration & Biosecurity → Duty / GST Settlement → Cargo Release
/ EDO → Final Delivery → Container Return (FCL)

A well-managed DDP shipment should identify who owns each step before
departure. Australian clearance documents should be prepared before
arrival wherever possible to reduce storage and container-related risk.

## 11. DDP quotation checklist

Before you compare two DDP quotations, request the following:

- Exact Incoterm: **DDP + named delivery place + Incoterms® 2020**
- Product name and HS/tariff classification basis
- Declared customs value
- Who is the owner/importer on the Australian declaration
- Customs broker / import-clearance structure
- Duty rate and GST treatment
- Whether the buyer can receive supporting import/GST documentation
- Biosecurity inclusions and exclusions
- Australian local charges included
- Delivery postcode and unloading requirements
- Demurrage, detention/container hire and storage exclusions
- Quote validity and expected transit time

## 12. Frequently asked questions

### What does DDP mean when shipping from China to Australia?

Under Incoterms 2020, Delivered Duty Paid places the broadest delivery
obligation on the seller. The seller arranges delivery to the named
destination, clears the goods for import and bears the agreed import
duties, taxes and customs formalities, while the goods are delivered
ready for unloading.

### Does DDP mean the Australian buyer has no import responsibilities?

Not necessarily. DDP allocates commercial responsibilities, but
Australian customs, tax, product-compliance and biosecurity rules still
apply. The parties must establish who is legally shown as owner/importer
and who can complete the required formalities.

### Who pays GST and duty under DDP to Australia?

Under a true DDP sale, these are commercially for the seller's account.
The legal payment and GST-credit position depends on the actual
Australian import structure.

### Can an Australian buyer claim an import GST credit on a DDP shipment?

It depends on who makes the creditable importation. If an unrelated
third party is the importer and simply passes the GST cost to the buyer,
the buyer should not assume it can claim that amount as an input tax
credit.

### Is DDP always better than DAP?

No. DAP can be cleaner for an established Australian importer that wants
to control its customs declaration, duty/GST position and broker
relationship.

### Does DDP cover biosecurity inspection and treatment?

Only if the commercial quotation says so. Australian biosecurity
requirements still apply, and unexpected inspection, treatment, storage
or permit costs should be allocated expressly in the agreement.

## Final recommendation

DDP can be a useful China-to-Australia shipping structure, but only when
the seller can explain the Australian import pathway clearly. Before
accepting the price, confirm the importer identity, customs broker
arrangement, GST-credit position, biosecurity scope, destination charges
and delivery point.
