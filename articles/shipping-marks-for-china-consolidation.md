If one supplier sends ten plain brown cartons to a warehouse, staff may identify the shipment from its delivery note. If eight suppliers deliver similar cartons on the same day, that assumption becomes risky. A package without a clear receiving reference can be difficult to match to the correct buyer, order or international shipment.

This is why freight forwarders often ask suppliers to place a **shipping mark** on every carton before delivery to a China consolidation warehouse.

A shipping mark is not decoration and it is not necessarily the final courier label. It is a practical package identifier that follows cargo through receiving, checking, storage, consolidation, customs preparation and dispatch.

This guide explains whether every carton needs a shipping mark, what it should contain and how overseas importers can prevent mixed, missing or delayed cargo when buying from multiple suppliers in China.

## Quick answer: does every carton need a shipping mark?

For a multi-carton or multi-supplier consolidation shipment, **the safest rule is to mark every outer carton or handling unit**. Each mark should contain a shipment-specific receiving reference and a carton sequence such as `1/12`, `2/12` through `12/12`.

A mark may be unnecessary only when the receiving warehouse has explicitly approved another identification method—for example, one sealed pallet that will never be broken down and already carries an unambiguous pallet label. Even then, individual cartons may still need identification if the pallet could be opened for inspection, counting, repacking or final delivery.

Do not assume that a supplier's domestic courier label is enough. Courier waybills can be damaged, removed or covered, and they normally identify only the local delivery—not the overseas buyer's consolidation order.

## What is a shipping mark?

A shipping mark is information printed, stencilled or labelled on the outside of a package so the parties handling it can identify its shipment and position within that shipment.

For China warehouse receiving, a simple mark may include:

- Importer or customer reference
- TengYoda receiving or warehouse code
- Purchase order or supplier reference
- Destination or project reference, if needed
- Carton number and total, such as `CTN 3/12`
- Product or SKU reference where useful
- Gross weight and dimensions where required
- Handling instructions supported by the actual package

The mark should be short enough to read quickly. It should not display unnecessary personal information, sensitive commercial details or a misleading destination.

## Why consolidation warehouses require marks

The warehouse receives physical packages, while the importer sees purchase orders, product lists and supplier chats. Shipping marks connect those two views.

### 1. Identify the correct customer and order

A freight warehouse may serve many overseas buyers. Supplier names are not enough because one factory can ship for several customers and one buyer may purchase from several trading companies. A unique receiving code and PO reference link the package to the correct warehouse record.

### 2. Count missing cartons

`1/12` through `12/12` tells the receiver that twelve cartons are expected. If only eleven arrive, the warehouse can identify the shortage before export instead of discovering it after delivery overseas.

### 3. Avoid mixing similar goods

Cartons of lighting, furniture fittings, spare parts, clothing or household goods can look identical outside. Without a reliable reference, staff may need to open cartons, contact suppliers and compare photos—a slow and sometimes inconclusive process.

### 4. Preserve identity during warehouse work

When pallets are opened, cartons measured or orders repacked, the original delivery arrangement changes. A mark on every carton preserves identity during inspection, consolidation and loading.

### 5. Support later investigation

If a carton is damaged, short-landed or delivered incorrectly, its number and shipment reference help the buyer, warehouse, forwarder and carrier investigate the same unit.

## One shipping mark per supplier is not enough

One common mistake is placing a mark on only the first carton. The cartons may arrive together, but they can be split during unloading, measurement, customs inspection or repacking.

Another mistake is attaching one paper mark to the stretch film around a pallet while leaving every carton blank. If the film is removed, the identity disappears.

For normal cartons, apply the approved mark to **each outer carton**. For pallets, crates, bundles, drums or machinery, mark each independent handling unit. Small retail units inside a sealed master carton normally do not need the warehouse receiving mark unless the agreed checking or fulfilment scope requires it.

## What should appear on a carton shipping mark?

The exact format should follow the warehouse's receiving instruction. A practical template is:

| Field | Example | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer/warehouse code | `TYD-AU-1842` | Links the package to the correct account |
| Purchase order | `PO-45810` | Matches cargo to the buyer's order |
| Supplier reference | `SUP-03` | Distinguishes factories in one consolidation |
| Destination | `BRISBANE` | Separates projects when instructed |
| Carton sequence | `CTN 3/12` | Shows package number and expected total |
| Product/SKU | `LAMP-07` | Helps checking similar orders |
| Gross weight | `G.W. 18 KG` | Supports handling and discrepancy checks |
| Dimensions | `55 × 35 × 45 CM` | Supports receiving and transport planning |

Not every field is mandatory. A unique warehouse reference plus carton numbering may be enough for a simple order. The important rule is consistency: the mark, packing list, supplier delivery record and warehouse instruction should refer to the same shipment.

## A simple shipping-mark template

The supplier can print the following information in large, dark text on white paper:

```text
WAREHOUSE CODE: TYD-AU-1842
PO: 45810
SUPPLIER: 03
DESTINATION: BRISBANE
CTN: 3/12
G.W.: 18 KG
MEAS.: 55 × 35 × 45 CM
```

This is an example, not a universal standard. Replace it with the exact reference issued for the shipment. Do not reuse an old warehouse code merely because the buyer and supplier are the same.

## Where and how should the mark be attached?

The label should remain visible and attached throughout normal handling.

- Use a flat, clean and dry outer surface.
- Print with strong contrast and a readable size.
- Avoid a carton seam or opening edge.
- Keep it away from corners likely to crush or tear.
- Do not cover product, safety or compliance labels.
- Use adhesive suitable for the carton, crate or plastic wrap.
- For long routes, place matching marks on two adjacent sides.
- Protect paper from rain or abrasion without hiding barcodes.
- Photograph the marked packages before dispatch.

Handwriting can work for a small shipment if it is clear, waterproof and consistent, but printed labels reduce mistakes. Do not write directly on retail packaging unless the buyer has approved it.

## Shipping mark versus courier, FBA and compliance labels

Several labels can appear on one carton, but they perform different jobs.

| Label type | Main purpose | Does it replace the warehouse mark? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warehouse receiving mark | Identifies the buyer, order and consolidation shipment | It is the mark specifically requested for receiving |
| Domestic courier waybill | Moves the package to the China warehouse | Usually no; it identifies local delivery only |
| International carrier label | Routes express or air cargo through a network | Usually applied after final service and pieces are confirmed |
| Marketplace/FBA carton label | Identifies cartons for a fulfilment inbound shipment | Not necessarily; the China warehouse may need its own code first |
| Product/SKU label | Identifies an individual saleable unit | No; it does not identify the outer receiving shipment |
| Dangerous-goods label | Communicates regulated transport information | No; this is a separate compliance requirement |
| Handling mark | Indicates orientation, fragility or lifting instructions | No; it describes handling, not order ownership |

Never cover or alter a dangerous-goods, battery, fumigation, certification or other required compliance mark. Do not use decorative “fragile” or “this way up” marks unless the packing genuinely supports the instruction.

## Do individual products inside the carton need marks?

Usually, the consolidation warehouse needs a mark on each **outer handling unit**, not every retail product inside it.

Individual labels may still be required when:

- The warehouse will pick, count or combine individual units.
- Several SKUs share one master carton.
- Goods are being prepared for marketplace fulfilment.
- The destination requires origin, product or safety labelling.
- Units will be delivered separately after deconsolidation.
- The importer requests serial-number or batch traceability.

This is a separate scope from basic consolidation. Tell the forwarder before arrival if the warehouse must open cartons, inspect units, attach SKU labels or prepare fulfilment cartons. Labour, materials and storage may be charged separately.

## Recommended multi-supplier consolidation workflow

### Step 1: create one supplier list

Record each supplier's company, contact, purchase order, product, package estimate and cargo-ready date. Assign a short supplier reference such as `SUP-01`, `SUP-02` and `SUP-03`.

### Step 2: request receiving instructions before dispatch

Do not let suppliers send cargo to an address copied from an old shipment. Confirm the current warehouse, contact, appointment rule, receiving code and required mark.

TengYoda has receiving options in Qingdao, Yiwu and Shenzhen, but the appropriate warehouse must be confirmed for the actual supplier locations and outbound route. The [China warehouse consolidation process](/blog/china-warehouse-consolidation-process/) explains these handoffs.

### Step 3: send the same format to every supplier

Ask each supplier to confirm final carton count, packed dimensions, gross weight, cargo-ready date, shipping-mark format, package photos and domestic delivery details.

### Step 4: receive and record each delivery

The warehouse matches the mark to its expected-delivery list, records received packages and flags shortages, visible damage or unidentified cargo.

### Step 5: resolve discrepancies before booking

Compare the packing list with the warehouse count and measurements. Clarify unknown cartons, repeated numbers and missing pieces while the supplier can still respond.

### Step 6: confirm final cargo and transport mode

Use measured volume, gross weight and package condition to compare express, air, LCL and FCL. The [China freight quote checklist](/blog/china-freight-quote-checklist/) lists the information needed for an accurate rate.

## Common shipping-mark mistakes

- **Reusing an old code:** it may send goods into a completed or wrong warehouse record.
- **Using only the buyer's name:** names can be duplicated or spelled differently; use the specific code.
- **Missing carton sequence:** number cartons `1/12` through `12/12` so shortages are traceable.
- **Different totals:** labels showing ten cartons while the packing list shows twelve create uncertainty.
- **Weak labels:** tiny text, blurred ink and poor adhesive are easily lost.
- **Dispatch before approval:** cargo may arrive without a valid contact, code or appointment.
- **One code for two destinations:** separate projects need separate references unless another control is approved.
- **Using the mark as a customs description:** a receiving code never replaces an accurate invoice and packing list.

## What happens when cartons arrive without marks?

The warehouse may hold the cargo in an unidentified area, search its domestic tracking record, ask for photos, inspect cartons if authorised, apply labels after identification, charge relabelling or storage, or refuse cargo that cannot be reliably identified.

Relabelling is not always simple. The warehouse must first prove which cartons belong to which order. Applying a new mark based on a guess can make the problem worse.

If unmarked goods are already moving, immediately send the forwarder the supplier name, driver or tracking number, package count, photos, delivery time and purchase-order details.

## Example: four suppliers, one overseas shipment

An Australian importer buys lighting, switches, furniture fittings and display materials from four factories. Each supplier sends brown cartons to one Shenzhen warehouse.

| Supplier | Receiving reference | Carton range | Warehouse action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lighting factory | `AU1842-S01` | `1/18–18/18` | Count and inspect visible condition |
| Switch supplier | `AU1842-S02` | `1/12–12/12` | Keep certificates with the order record |
| Fittings supplier | `AU1842-S03` | `1/10–10/10` | Verify small cartons remain sealed |
| Display supplier | `AU1842-S04` | `1/6–6/6` | Check dimensions before mode comparison |

The warehouse can report forty-six received cartons, identify shortages by supplier and produce one final packing record for international dispatch.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a shipping mark legally required on every export carton?

Not every ordinary shipment has one universal legal carton-mark format. However, warehouses, carriers, buyers, dangerous-goods rules or destination regulations may impose specific requirements. For consolidation, marking every handling unit is a practical risk-control rule.

### Can I use my company logo as the shipping mark?

A logo alone is usually insufficient because it does not identify the order, warehouse record or carton number. Use the requested receiving reference; add a logo only if appropriate.

### Can the warehouse attach marks after receiving?

Possibly, if it offers the service and can reliably identify the cargo. Confirm the work and cost in advance. Supplier-applied marks are normally faster and reduce receiving uncertainty.

### Do pallets need marks if every carton is labelled?

Usually yes. Add a pallet-level shipment reference and pallet sequence while keeping carton labels visible where possible.

### Are “Made in China” labels the same as shipping marks?

No. Country-of-origin marking identifies product origin. A shipping mark identifies the shipment, order and package. Destination origin-marking rules should be checked separately.

### What if the carton count changes after labels are printed?

Reprint affected marks and update the packing list so the sequence and total agree. Do not leave duplicated carton numbers or an unexplained gap.

## Final checklist before warehouse delivery

- The warehouse address and receiving code are current.
- Every outer carton or handling unit carries the approved mark.
- Buyer, order and supplier references are consistent.
- Cartons have a unique sequence and accurate total.
- Marks are readable, durable and away from seams.
- Domestic delivery details are shared.
- The packing list matches the marked packages.
- Special handling and compliance labels remain visible.
- Photos are taken before dispatch.
- The warehouse knows what is expected and when it will arrive.

Shipping marks are a small preparation step, but they become especially valuable when many suppliers, cartons and handoffs are involved. A clear mark on every handling unit gives the warehouse a reliable way to receive the right goods into the right shipment.
