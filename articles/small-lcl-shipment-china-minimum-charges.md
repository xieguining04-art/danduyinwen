A small LCL shipment from China can cost more than expected because the invoice includes more than the ocean rate. Minimum billable quantities, shipment-level documentation fees, warehouse handling, destination deconsolidation and final delivery can outweigh the sea freight itself. A low price per cubic metre is therefore not enough to decide whether LCL is your cheapest option.

For an overseas importer shipping a few cartons or one small pallet, the useful question is: **What will it cost to move these packed goods from this supplier to this delivery address, including every confirmed minimum and local charge?**

## Why a few cartons still require a full logistics process

LCL means less-than-container load: your goods share container space with other shipments. You buy part of that space, but your order still needs identification, receiving, transport documents, export coordination and a destination handoff.

At origin, cargo may be collected from the factory, measured, weighed and received at a consolidation warehouse. At destination, the container is unpacked at a container freight station (CFS), individual shipments are separated, and your goods are released for onward delivery after the applicable requirements are satisfied.

Some work increases with cargo size. Other work happens once for each shipment, even when you send only a few boxes. That is why halving the volume does not usually halve the total invoice.

## What does an LCL minimum charge mean?

A minimum charge sets the lowest billable quantity or amount for a particular service. For example, if a quoted ocean rate is USD 80 per W/M with a minimum of 1 W/M, a shipment assessed at 0.40 W/M would still incur USD 80 for that line.

This does **not** mean every LCL service worldwide has the same minimum, or that every invoice line uses the ocean freight minimum. A quotation might apply one minimum to sea freight, another to CFS handling, and a fixed amount to documentation. Ask for the unit and minimum on each charge.

DHL's published regional forwarding terms provide an example of LCL charges subject to a 1 cubic metre minimum unless otherwise specified. This is evidence of how minimums can be structured, not a universal tariff or a TengYoda rate. Your route-specific written quotation controls the calculation.

## CBM, W/M and revenue tonnes: what are you actually paying for?

CBM means cubic metres. Calculate packed volume using the outside dimensions of every package, including pallets or crates:

**Length in metres × width in metres × height in metres × number of identical packages.**

For example, eight cartons measuring 0.50 × 0.40 × 0.30 metres each total 0.48 CBM before any pallet or additional protection is added.

W/M means weight or measurement. In a common ocean LCL arrangement, 1 CBM is compared with 1 metric tonne, and the higher quantity is used. This convention must be confirmed with the provider; do not apply it to air freight or express shipments, which use their own chargeable-weight rules.

Under that example convention:

| Packed volume | Gross weight | W/M before any minimum | Explanation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.48 CBM | 120 kg | 0.48 | Volume is higher than 0.12 tonnes |
| 0.48 CBM | 900 kg | 0.90 | Weight is higher than volume |
| 0.48 CBM | 1,300 kg | 1.30 | Dense cargo is charged by weight |

If the relevant line has a minimum of 1 W/M, the first two examples would be billed at 1 W/M. The third would be billed at 1.30 W/M, subject to any stated rounding rules and cargo acceptance conditions.

Heavy, long, fragile or non-stackable packages can require special handling or a different assessment. Confirm this before the supplier dispatches them.

## Which fees make a small LCL shipment expensive?

The quotation should separate costs by stage and billing basis. The following are possible categories, not a list of fees that every shipment necessarily incurs.

| Stage | Possible charge | What to confirm |
|---|---|---|
| China collection | Factory pickup or domestic delivery | Address, access, loading and any minimum trip charge |
| Origin warehouse | Receiving, CFS handling, measurement or packing | Per CBM, per W/M, per package or fixed; minimum quantity |
| Export documents | Documentation and export declaration coordination | Per shipment, per bill of lading or per declaration |
| Ocean transport | Freight and applicable surcharges | W/M convention, minimum, validity and routing |
| Destination warehouse | Deconsolidation and CFS handling | Local tariff, minimum and payment recipient |
| Cargo release | Document or release-related charges | What is required and whether the fee is included |
| Import clearance | Brokerage and applicable government charges | Included services; duties and taxes listed separately |
| Final delivery | Trucking and access-related services | Postcode, business or residential address, unloading |

Storage, inspection, repacking and other conditional expenses should be identified separately. Ask when free storage starts and ends and what triggers extra charges.

## A worked example: why USD 80 per CBM is not your delivered price

The following numbers are **invented solely to explain the arithmetic**. They are not current market prices, a destination tariff or a TengYoda quotation. Assume 0.40 CBM, 100 kg of ordinary stackable cargo, and a 1 W/M minimum on the two variable lines shown.

| Illustrative line | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Ocean freight | USD 80 × minimum 1 W/M | USD 80 |
| Origin handling | USD 35 × minimum 1 W/M | USD 35 |
| Documentation | Fixed per shipment | USD 45 |
| Factory pickup | Assumed fixed trip | USD 60 |
| Destination handling and release | Assumed combined fixed allowance | USD 150 |
| Brokerage service | Assumed fixed service fee | USD 70 |
| Final delivery | Assumed fixed trip | USD 90 |
| **Illustrative transport and service total** | **Sum of the listed amounts** | **USD 530** |

The calculation excludes duties, taxes, insurance, inspections, storage and any unlisted service. Real invoices may use multiple currencies and different billing bases.

Multiplying 0.40 CBM by USD 80 would produce USD 32, but that overlooks the ocean minimum and the other stages. Conversely, dividing USD 530 by 0.40 produces USD 1,325 per actual CBM; that figure reflects fixed expenses spread over a tiny shipment, not an ocean rate of USD 1,325 per CBM.

## Can consolidation reduce the cost?

Combining compatible orders from several suppliers can spread some shipment-level costs over more goods. TengYoda can coordinate China pickups, receiving references, packed measurements and a common dispatch plan before comparing transport options.

However, consolidation does not automatically remove minimums or combine every charge. Goods with different destinations, consignees, documentation requirements or handling restrictions may need separate processing. Additional pickups, storage, checks and repacking also cost money.

Ask whether the expected saving is greater than the extra collection and warehouse costs. ## Should you use express, air freight or LCL for a small order?

There is no universal CBM threshold at which LCL becomes cheapest. Compare actual quotations for the same goods, address and service scope.

- **Express:** worth checking for samples and a few manageable cartons. Review dimensional weight, oversize surcharges, destination coverage and import charges.
- **Air freight:** worth checking when urgency, cargo value or weight makes faster transport useful. Include airport handling, brokerage and onward delivery.
- **LCL:** worth checking for larger or heavier orders that can tolerate the consolidation timetable. Include minimums and destination fees.

Do not compare a port-to-port LCL rate with a courier door-delivery price. Ask for the same endpoints, customs responsibilities and exclusions. ## Seven questions to ask before approving a quotation

1. What are the final packed dimensions, total CBM and gross weight?
2. Which lines use CBM or W/M, and what weight-to-volume convention applies?
3. What minimum and rounding rule applies to each variable charge?
4. Which amounts are fixed per shipment, document or declaration?
5. What destination charges are confirmed, estimated or excluded, and who collects them?
6. Does delivery include unloading, appointments and the actual destination postcode?
7. What is the quotation validity, and what could change the agreed amount?

If a bill is higher than expected, request an itemised explanation and measurement evidence before assuming the cause. A minimum, new pallet dimensions, different delivery scope or an omitted destination charge are different issues. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I ship less than 1 CBM by LCL?

Often yes, subject to service and cargo acceptance. The actual volume can be below the billable minimum. Confirm the minimum on each fee rather than assuming that every line is charged at actual volume.

### Does a minimum of 1 CBM mean I should add more goods?

Only if you need the goods and the complete cost supports it. Adding cargo can change weight-based billing, packing, handling, tax and delivery expenses. Unnecessary inventory can cost more than any freight saving.

### Are destination charges included in the sea freight rate?

Not necessarily. Ask for written inclusion and exclusion details. “Prepaid freight” alone does not establish that every destination or delivery charge has been paid.

### Will shipping without a pallet be cheaper?

A pallet can increase outside dimensions but improve handling and protection. Ask the warehouse and transport provider to assess safe packing, stackability and acceptance before removing it.

### Can TengYoda guarantee express will be cheaper than LCL?

No. The result depends on dimensions, weight, product acceptance, destination and service scope. A useful recommendation comes from comparing like-for-like quotations.
